Why Depth Is Everything

The Difference Between
Waist Deep and Chest Deep

TheraTank is the only pool that offers actual chest-deep water — 59" walls, 54"+ of usable depth, so even at 6 feet tall you're still at therapeutic depth. At waist depth, you offload 50% of your body weight; your hips, spine, and core are still loading — and for someone with chronic pain or post-surgical joints, that other 50% is still the problem. At chest depth, that offload reaches 75%, your joints barely register the load, and movement that felt impossible becomes available again.

That depth also means your arms and legs move fully underwater, so you can work your whole body — suspension training and full-body workouts that used to require a lap pool or a clinic, now in your backyard.

Patients with chronic low back pain were 6.6x more likely to achieve meaningful pain relief with aquatic therapy than standard physical therapy — and results held at 12 months. Disability reduction: 46.4% vs. 7%.

Rivas Neira et al. (2024), Physiotherapy

Post-Surgical Recovery

Aquatic therapy provides a supportive and low-impact environment that can significantly aid in the recovery process, helping patients regain function and return to their daily activities.

Neurological Rehabilitation

A 2025 review of 27 studies and more than 1,100 stroke survivors found that aquatic therapy significantly improved balance, walking speed, and fall risk. Water supports the body while you practice standing, stepping, and shifting your weight, so you can work on the movements that matter without fear of falling.
Gento-Andres et al. (2025), Arch. Rehabil. Res. Clin. Transl.

Athletic Injury Recovery

Deep water training allows patients recovering from ACL tears, rotator cuff injuries, and stress fractures to maintain cardiovascular conditioning and muscle engagement while completely offloading healing tissue — zero ground impact.
Established sports medicine protocol

Exercise Soreness & Recovery

If soreness keeps knocking you out of your routine, the problem may be the exercise, not you. On land, the lengthening (eccentric) phase of each movement causes the micro-tears behind delayed-onset muscle soreness. In water, drag replaces gravity as the resistance, which largely removes that eccentric load. You can work hard today and be ready for whatever tomorrow brings.

Balance & Fall Prevention

If fear of falling keeps you from training balance, water takes the risk out. Its viscosity slows every movement and buoyancy catches you if you lose your footing. You can challenge your balance and proprioception (your body's sense of where it is in space) further than you safely could on land, building the stability that keeps you upright out of the pool.
Oh & Lee (2021), J. Back & Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation

The Science

Why Water Works Differently

10 physics-based effects exclusive to water — impossible to replicate on land.

Continuous Lymphatic Pumping

Hydrostatic pressure acts like a full-body compression garment — actively squeezing interstitial fluids out of limbs to reduce swelling and inflammation. Unlike compression socks or sleeves, this effect is three-dimensional and continuous.

Gravitational Offloading (Buoyancy)

At chest depth, buoyancy counteracts approximately 75% of your body weight — removing gravitational compression from joints, spinal discs, and connective tissue during exercise. This is what makes aquatic exercise possible for people who cannot exercise on land without pain. It is physically impossible to replicate outside of water.

Enhanced Venous Return

Water pressure forces pooled blood from your lower extremities back to the heart, increasing stroke volume and cardiovascular efficiency. Your heart works smarter, not harder.

Concentric-Only Training

Fluid drag replaces gravity as resistance. This eliminates eccentric (lengthening) muscle contractions — the phase responsible for micro-tears and delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS). Aquatic exercise lets you work hard and wake up ready.

Balanced Agonist / Antagonist Work

Water pushes back in every direction. That means opposing muscle pairs — biceps and triceps, quads and hamstrings — are engaged simultaneously. The result is more symmetrical strength development than most land-based training allows.

Thicker Fiber Hypertrophy

Concentric-focused resistance builds muscle volume by increasing fiber thickness (myofibrillar hypertrophy) rather than fiber length. This means functional, dense strength gains with less recovery demand.

Pulmonary Conditioning

Hydrostatic pressure compresses the chest, forcing your respiratory muscles to work up to 60% harder just to expand your lungs. Regular aquatic exercise measurably improves respiratory muscle strength — a unique benefit unavailable on any treadmill.

Accelerated Heat Dissipation

Water conducts heat 25× faster than air. Your core temperature stays lower during aquatic exercise, dramatically reducing sweat-induced dehydration and heat exhaustion risk — especially valuable for older adults and those with chronic illness.

Thermodynamic Calorie Boost

When water temperature is slightly below body temperature, your metabolism works harder to maintain core heat while exercising. The result: more calories burned per session compared to the same effort performed on land.

Proprioceptive Training

Water presses on every inch of submerged skin and shifts with every move you make. That constant pressure and turbulence feeds a steady stream of sensory input to the receptors in your skin, muscles, and joints, while your stabilizing muscles make continuous micro-corrections to keep you upright. The result is sharper body awareness and coordination — the foundation of balance.

The Fitmax Footprint Problem
The Fitmax iPool advertises a 7x7 footprint but uses angled buttress legs that protrude outward, requiring a true 9x10 foot ground space. PT Pools uses vertical straight-down legs (Intex Prism/Ultra frame style) with hoop-stress tension along the top rail — delivering a true 8-foot circular footprint with no outward protrusion.
This is a factual, ethical competitive claim we can make in all marketing.



6. Founder Story
The TherapyPod exists because the founder tried to buy one and couldn't.

Looking for a true aquatic therapy pool for home use, the search kept hitting the same two walls: the Fitmax iPool at $1,799 advertised as a 7-foot pool — but requiring 9-10 feet of actual ground space due to its angled outward legs. The perfect location in the garden had electric, had sun, had the right dimensions. Except the Fitmax legs would have blocked the greenhouse door. The spot was lost.

The fallback was a 6.5x13 foot Intex at 48 inches deep — functional, but a compromise. Too shallow for true therapeutic depth without scooting down in the water. And the only available location was shaded — meaning a potential $5,000 electrical line just to run a heater properly.

Meanwhile the husband — an Ironman triathlete — needed a swim lane. So the therapy pool became a shared swim lane. Two people, two completely different needs, one pool that half-served both.

The TherapyPod by PT Pools is the pool that should have existed. True 7-foot footprint that fits through a greenhouse door. True 54-inch therapeutic depth. Small enough volume that a plug-in heater handles it without an electrician. Long enough in the SwimLane configuration for a triathlete to train. All-in pricing with no freight surprises. And a companion app and HSA pathway that no competitor has even considered.

Built by someone who lived every one of these frustrations personally. That's not a marketing angle. That's the product.

Logo design for a company called "PT Pools" — a premium aquatic therapy pool brand. Clean, minimal, modern. The logo should feel professional and trustworthy but not cold or overly medical.
